> なんておハレンチな姿でしょう！アイラにそんなお姿を見せつけになるなんて、もうお婚約がお前提って事なのですね！よっしゃあ！

> Oh, my! What a vulgar sight! That you would flaunt your body to me in such an intimate display… I can only assume the engagement ring will be forthcoming! Fuck yeah!

I found this line interesting because Aira is mostly in her regular form, but is talking like Silcrobat (extraneous honorific おs, archaic and aristocratic speech pattern). Silcrobat’s speech is fun to translate, but I do worry slightly that it sounds a bit animenglish. Not much I can do when it’s built into the script though!

One other notable thing about this line is the jarring transition from that aristocratic speech pattern to an unrestrained and almost vulgar exclamation. While the Japanese よっしゃあ！ is not vulgar itself, it conveys a sense of forgetting oneself in an emotional outburst and I felt like the only way to translate the tonal whiplash was to use vulgar language.

![A yellow-tinted creature seen from behind, its body covered in black maze-like stripes. The subtitle reads "♪ Black and yellow ♪"](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-9/image-2.png)

> ♪ Black and yellow ♪ \
> ♪ Is the mark of a fighter ♪ \
> ♪ Can you soldier on for 24 hours? ♪ \
> ♪ Regain™, Regain™ ♪ \
> ♪ Our own Regain™ ♪

This is a very explicit reference to the old advertisements for the energy drink Regain. This is quite a strange scene if you don’t recognise the reference, so I tried to make it clear that it was a reference by using the TM symbol to imply that Regain is a real product. Here’s the ad: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPYZCgKPFnE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPYZCgKPFnE)

![A glowing green scene of a hulking alien with red eyes looming over a small figure flying through beams of yellow light. The subtitle reads "We may look like this, but we are a close-combat in-fighter UMA."](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-9/image-3.png)

(The above screenshot is from the Netflix subs.)

I want to also make a note about the Japanese word “UMA” (also written ユーマ), because the Netflix subs leave it completely as-is even though it’s not an English word. UMA is an acronym of the English “Unidentified Mysterious Animal”, though this appears to be an example of 和製英語 (wasei-eigo: Japanese-made English). Much like any 和製英語, it would be a grave sin to translate it directly. For example: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eSqBUiq2A2w](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eSqBUiq2A2w)

> 山本さんの家、どんな家？ \
> うちはマンションだよ

> What kind of house do you live in, Yamamoto-san? \
> My family lives in a *mansion*.

Vs. the actual translation:

> What kind of house do you live in, Yamamoto-san? \
> My family lives in an *apartment*.

English speakers won’t understand the word UMA, and there’s already a direct translation for it which evokes the themes of Dandadan much better anyway: Cryptid! If you go to the [Wikipedia article for UMA](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%AA%E7%A2%BA%E8%AA%8D%E7%94%9F%E7%89%A9%E4%B8%80%E8%A6%A7) and switch languages to English, you’ll get an article called “List of cryptids”! I've used this translation in later episodes. However, for this line, it made more sense to rephrase the line entirely.
