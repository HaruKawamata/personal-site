A lot of series-spanning decisions have to be made in the first episode of any anime translation. Let’s look at some of the more important ones before we move onto the episode-specific translation notes:

**What does Okarun call Momo?** In Japanese, he calls her 綾瀬さん (Ayase-san). This is a very common way for high schoolers to refer to each other, especially if they aren’t super close. And the most common way in English for high schoolers to refer to each other is simply by their first name. So, for example, I’ve translated Aira’s 高倉くん (Takakura-kun) as “Ken”.

However, Okarun’s case is slightly different. His discomfort, unfamiliarity, and anxiety in having a friend for the first time need to be emphasised in his form of address, and he continues calling Momo 綾瀬さん (Ayase-san) well into the series, even when they are much closer and he is more practiced in the ways of friendship. There’s even a scene (that the anime hasn’t caught up to yet) which makes this extremely plot relevant. I needed to choose something unusual, but not unnatural. The most common translation, “Miss Ayase”, was completely out of the question. Students simply never refer to each other by titles like this, even nerds who’ve never had a friend in their life. I settled on “Momo Ayase” — using her full name each time he refers to her. This was a tricky decision because Japanese simply has many more natural, in-use options for addressing someone than English does. The full name option sounds nerdy and weird, and is certainly something that can be remarked upon in that scene mentioned above, but it doesn’t sound completely unrealistic like “Miss Ayase” does. I’ll have more to say on this when Aira comes into the picture, but this is already long enough!

**What is the name of the speedy old lady spirit?** You may have seen my polls on this topic: [1](https://www.tumblr.com/haru-dipthong/800755660271861760/im-currently-translating-dandadan-and-ive-been?source=share), [2](https://www.tumblr.com/haru-dipthong/804007438422622208?source=share). Obviously, the official translation is “Turbo Granny”, but I hate this for multiple reasons as I explained in the polls:

- It sounds goofy, not scary
- It doesn’t sound like the name of an urban legend
- ババア is a derogatory form of “old lady” and this should come across in the English
- Using a familial relation term generically sounds weird in English

I tried out so many options, even seeing how they looked when put into the subs, but the best one in the end was Highway Hag.

**How do the kids talk?** In Japanese, the kids talk like kids! They use slang, they speak in maximally casual registers (excepting Okarun and Aira), and they use their language to signal membership of a group. Surprisingly, the *dubs* do a pretty good, sometimes fantastic, job of translating this, while the subs are god-awful. Check out this scene right after Momo and her boyfriend break up:

> Dub Kei: Like, wow. Calling him seafood is pretty lame.\
> Dub Momo: Cut me some slack! My first boyfriend just broke up with me!

> Sub Kei: Totes funny! Seafood names aren’t insults.\
> Sub Momo: Come on, comfort me! He was my first boyfriend.

Whoever did the subs *recognised* that the kids were speaking in a casual teenage register, but was obviously too old or disconnected to come up with modern, realistic English equivalents.

What this means for my translation is that the kids will talk like kids. They will casually swear, like teenagers do (the show has several near-rape scenes, so I think the audience can handle a couple of fucks and shits). There will be some intentional grammatical “mistakes” if it serves to make the dialogue more realistic. For example, in the bad sub scene from above, “Seafood names aren’t insults” sounds odd because it’s trying too hard to be grammatical. And the slang I use will be modern, but I’ll try not to use slang that will become outdated too quickly. In fact, I asked a young coworker of mine to help me out with a list of slang phrases and words I could use in the translation.

This is already long, but I wanted to single out two scenes in this episode to talk about the specific lines.

![Screenshot from Ep 1 of the Dandadan anime. It's a full frame close up of ターボババア. Her shaggy white hair frames her grey-green wrinkled face, and her eyes are pointing in extreme opposite directions, giving the impression of two blank eyes.](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-1/image-2.png)

> おっぱい吸わせてやるからよ。 いちもつしゃぶらせろ。

> I’ll let you suck on my tits. So you better get that juicy cock out.

It’s vitally important that this scene is as creepy as possible. The other important aspects to convey are that the Highway Hag considers this an even trade (吸わせてやるから), and also that Okarun is getting his dick sucked whether he likes it or not (しゃぶらせろ). Highway Hag is using the causative-imperative form, which makes it a command (“Let me suck it”) rather than a request, but “let me” still sounds slightly deferential compared to the causative form of “suck” used in the Japanese. This is why I made it a command to “get it out” instead of “let me suck it” — it sounds much more forceful and frightening which matches the Japanese. The problem with this choice is that it’s not 100% clear what the Hag is going to do with the cock once it’s out, which is why I added “juicy” as a descriptor, which heavily implies a desire to suck it.

![Screenshot from Ep 1 of the Dandadan anime. A Serpoian in human disguise poses unsettlingly with its enormous surgical penis machine protruding out of its pants. It has an uncanny human-like face and is wearing standard male office worker clothes.](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-1/image-3.png)

> 今からセックスします。あなたとセックスした後、子宮やバナナ摘出して研究します。

> We will now have “sex”. After we have “sex”, we will surgically remove your womb and banana organ.

> むらむらしてもらうためにサイコキネシスを脳波をコントロールします。

> In order to make you horny for us, we will use psychokinesis to control your brain waves.

The Serpoians are the clearest and most explicit depiction of the themes of rape and sexual assault in the series. The way they look, the way they talk… I think Dandadan is trying to say “Sexual assault is horrifying because it’s not as alien as you think. It’s horrifying how real it is, it’s horrifying how mundane the contexts of assault can be. Or how there will always be pieces of mundanity amongst the horror.” The Serpoians disguise themselves as salarymen, the most mundane genre of men. They speak in a mundane formal register of Japanese. They are emotionless in their attempted rape. (Ever notice how they take off their disguises and become truly alien only after scary assault scenes turn into shonen-style fight scenes?)

I chose to use the word “horny” because in Japanese it’s むらむら, a word that’s decidedly non-clinical in stark contrast to the rest of their very clinical language. The Serpoians speak like they learned Japanese exclusively from corporate language guides and porn site comments.
