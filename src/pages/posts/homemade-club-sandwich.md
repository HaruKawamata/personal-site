I made a sandwich at home the other day, and it turned out really well so I thought I’d post my recipe here.

The recipe is a modified club sandwich. It differs from the traditional model in that I did not use a middle slice of bread, and I used a cheese crisp instead of bacon. The cheese crisp provides essentially the same umami bomb and crunch factor as bacon does, but it’s a little less carcinogenic and (in my opinion) tastier.

I find club sandwiches to be better with fresh, untoasted bread. A sandwich with lettuce usually doesn’t do well toasted on a press or in a pan because the leaves wilt under the heat and lose their crunch, which is basically the whole point of it. 

Unfortunately the only bread I had was refrigerated, so it needed a bit of heat to freshen it up. I also wanted to pump up the flavour levels since the contents aren’t too fatty and salty themselves. So rather than toasting the entire assembled sandwich, I toasted the bread individually in a hot pan with butter on one side, and I only very lightly toasted it, nude, on the other side. This technique preserves fluffiness, allows internal soakage of sauce and chicken juice, and avoids bread that’s so crunchy that it tears up the roof of your mouth.

For the slow roasted chicken:

- 2 chicken breasts
- 2 (or more) cloves of garlic
- The juice of 1 lemon and an equal amount of olive oil

For the rest of the sandwich:

- 2 slices of bread
- 1 teaspoon butter
- A handful of shredded pizza cheese
- Oakleaf lettuce
- 1 Tomato
- Mayonnaise
- Dijon mustard

Process:

1. Preheat the oven to 120 C. Mix the lemon juice and olive oil together. Finely chop the garlic and add it to the mixture. Add 1 teaspoon of salt and several cranks of pepper. Then whisk with a fork.
2. Put the chicken breasts in a small baking tray, then pour the lemon and oil mixture over the top of them. Make sure all of the chicken gets lubricated with it. Cover in alfoil and put in the oven for 1 hour, occasionally taking it out to baste the chicken in the juices.
3. When the chicken is almost done, put a pan on medium heat and add a handful of cheese to the middle. It will melt, and then start frying itself. Once it looks quite browned on one side, flip it over and fry it briefly before transferring it to a plate with a paper towel.
4. Put the butter in the pan and then add the two slices of bread. Move them around so they soak up the butter. When sufficiently toasted, flip briefly to the other side before transferring to a plate. The buttered, more toasted sides will go on the outside.
5. When the chicken is done, take it out and put it on a chopping board to rest. Slice it against the grain so it’s more tender to bite into (and so you don’t pull out the entire slice every time you take a bite).
6. Spread mayo and mustard on the bottom slice of bread. Then add some slices of tomato, a pinch of salt and pepper, then the chicken, then lettuce, then the cheese crisp, and then another layer of mayo and mustard on the top slice of bread. You can add any other sauces you like (I tried it again with buffalo sauce and blue cheese sauce and it was really good).

4/4!
