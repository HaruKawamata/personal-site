![Nanami, in a dark dress with a yellow bow, stands glaring at a large grey box with two straps in a gloomy setting. The subtitle reads "You got between me and my brother."](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-10/image-1.png)

The start of this episode where Saionji is expelled has a lot of very Japanese talk of shame, responsibility, 面倒 (*mendou*: care/burden), etc. Many of the turns of phrase sound funny when directly translated, so it makes more sense to rephase the sentence into something more familiar. Here’s a lightning round of 3 lines and their various translations. Hopefully based on the colour coding you’ll be able to see where and why I made the choices I did.

> <span class="color-orange">破廉恥</span><span class="color-blue">にもほどがある</span>

> <span class="color-blue">Is there any limit to his</span> <span class="color-orange">shame</span>? (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> <span class="color-blue">Is there no limit to his</span> <span class="color-orange">shamelessness</span>? (from internet archive)

> <span class="color-blue">How</span> <span class="color-orange">desperate</span> <span class="color-blue">can you get</span>? (my translation)

Said by Juri. <span class="color-orange">破廉恥</span> means “shameless, disgraceful”. <span class="color-blue">にもほどがある</span> kind of means “<span class="color-blue">it’s an understatement to say X</span>” or “<span class="color-blue">is there a limit to X?</span>”. I think talking of “<span class="color-orange">shame</span>” in this sense is distinctly Japanese. If Juri was an English speaker I think she would come at the behaviour from a different angle — desperation.

> <span class="color-green">この上</span>、<span class="color-pink">面倒かけて</span><span class="color-blue">悪いんだが</span>…

> <span class="color-blue">Sorry to</span> <span class="color-green">further</span> <span class="color-pink">burden you</span>… (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> <span class="color-blue">I’m sorry</span> <span class="color-blue">for</span> <span class="color-pink">all the trouble I’ve caused you</span>. (from internet archive)

> <span class="color-blue">I hate to</span> <span class="color-pink">ask</span> <span class="color-green">another</span> <span class="color-pink">favour of you,</span> <span class="color-blue">but</span>… (my translation)

This from Saionji — a literal translation would be “on top of everything, I’m sorry for burdening you with another thing”. This is just before he shows Touga the exchange diary to give back to Anthy. Interesting to me that the Internet Archive subs don’t mention that he’s asking a new favour.

> <span class="color-purple">俺は</span><span class="color-red">責任持って</span><span class="color-blue">渡しておくよ</span>

> <span class="color-purple">I’ll</span> <span class="color-red">take responsibility for</span> <span class="color-blue">delivering it to her</span>. (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> <span class="color-purple">I’ll</span> <span class="color-red">take care of it for you</span>. (from internet archive)

> <span class="color-purple">I’ll</span> <span class="color-red">personally see to it that</span> <span class="color-blue">she receives this</span>. (my translation)

<span class="color-red">責任持って</span> literally means “<span class="color-red">hold onto the responsibility and</span>…” which sounds better as “<span class="color-red">take responsibility for</span>…” but I think it sounds even better completely rephrased. Side note: The Internet Archive subs make another interesting decision here. “<span class="color-red">Take care of</span>” is ambiguous and makes it so that Touga isn’t outright lying (since he immediately incinerates the diary). But he literally does lie in Japanese, he says he’s going to hand it over.

—–

> <span class="color-pink">やはり</span>、女の子は王子様にはなれないのでしょうか。

> <span class="color-pink">Should have known</span>, a girl can’t become a prince… (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> <span class="color-pink">Maybe</span> a girl <span class="color-pink">really</span> can’t become a prince… (from internet archive)

> <span class="color-pink">Maybe</span> girls can’t be princes <span class="color-pink">after all</span>. (my translation)

A classic translation foible: translating <span class="color-pink">やはり</span> as “<span class="color-pink">as expected</span>” or “<span class="color-pink">I should have known</span>”. Of course it can sometimes be translated as that, but in many contexts that translation doesn’t make sense. My vibe read of <span class="color-pink">やはり</span> is that it signifies that the speaker is reflecting back to a past state of mind. In some cases, this is thinking back and realising you were right all along, hence “<span class="color-pink">as expected</span>” as a common translation. But in some cases, like this one, you’re thinking back and reconsidering that you may have been wrong. I guess this is what the “<span class="color-pink">should have known</span>” translation is trying to do, but it’s not as strong as that. The English phrase “<span class="color-pink">should have known</span>” is almost berating yourself for not knowing, whereas the Japanese <span class="color-pink">やはり</span> is not nearly as self-deprecating. It just shows that you’re reflecting and reconsidering.

—–

> <span class="color-orange">目障り</span>なのよあんた達。いつもお兄様の周りに<span class="color-blue">うろちょろと</span>。

> You two are an <span class="color-orange">eyesore</span>, always <span class="color-blue">hovering</span> around my brother. (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> You’re both <span class="color-orange">eyesores</span>, always <span class="color-blue">hanging</span> around my brother. (from internet archive)

> You two <span class="color-orange">sicken me</span>. Always <span class="color-blue">dangling off</span> my brother <span class="color-blue">like parasites</span>. (my translation)

It’s sad that these other translations used “<span class="color-orange">eyesore</span>”. Technically that’s what the word means, but I’m being completely honest when I say I’ve literally never seen or heard the word “<span class="color-orange">eyesore</span>” outside of an anime translation. I really, *really* hate the word “<span class="color-orange">eyesore</span>”. In my mind it’s the very essence of poor translation.

What does <span class="color-orange">目障り</span> actually *mean*? It’s a compound word — 目 is “eye” and 障り is “hinderance”, “obstruction” or “sickness”. It basically just means “<span class="color-orange">I can’t stand the sight of you</span>”. So I translated it as such: if someone “<span class="color-orange">sickens you</span>” then you can’t stand the sight of them. This plays into the second half of the line.

I took a few liberties with the second half. <span class="color-blue">うろちょろ</span> is an onomatopoeic word for like, pottering around, loitering, hovering without doing anything. I actually like this part of the translation from the ohtori script, “<span class="color-blue">hovering</span>” is a great word. お兄様の周り can mean “around my brother” or “in the vicinity of my brother”. I believe Nanami is trying to get across the idea that Utena and Anthy have nothing better to do than hover around Touga. The problem was that the “<span class="color-blue">hovering around</span>” phrasing didn’t feel visceral enough to go with “<span class="color-orange">sicken me</span>”. I could have reworded the first half (”<span class="color-orange">I can’t stand you two</span>”), but I really liked how emotionally charged the line felt, so I doubled down on it.
