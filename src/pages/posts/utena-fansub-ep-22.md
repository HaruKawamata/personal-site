![Nanami, in her yellow student council uniform, frowns at Miki, whose blue-haired profile fills the foreground against a red background. The subtitle reads "I wish you'd put your little birdies to work finding out just who is behind this."](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-22/image-1.png)

> さすがに何でもよく知ってるわね。でもそれだけの情報網があるなら、早く奴らの正体も突き止めて欲しいわ。

> You know everything, don’t you Juri? With such a vast intelligence network, I wish you’d hurry up and find out these people’s true identities. (literal translation)

> You know everything, don’t you Juri? I wish you’d put your little birdies to work finding out just *who* is behind this. (final translation)

情報網 (jouhoumou) is an interesting word to use here! It literally means “intelligence network”, so Nanami is implying that Juri has a bunch of spies feeding her everytthing that happens at the school. To actually use the word “intelligence network” sounds a little but out of place in English though, so I rephrased the line a little to sound more conversational and accusatory.

![A painted view through a dark arched doorway of a tall, ornate stone building against a yellow sky. The subtitle reads "Won't disappear for a while still, eh, Professor Nemuro?"](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-22/image-2.png)

> Boy: しばらく消えないでしょうね、根室教授。 \
> Nemuro: ん？なんだい？\
> Boy: 雪の事ですよ、教授。

> Boy: Won’t disappear for a while still, eh, Professor Nemuro? Nemuro: I beg your pardon?\
> Boy: I was talking about the snow, Professor.

There’s a double meaning in the first line here — the boy could reasonably be talking about the Professor himself not clearing out for a while. That was actually my original phrasing: “Won’t clear out for a while” but Anya suggested “disappear” which is actually much closer to the original Japanese phrasing so I changed it to that. I think this exchange preserves that passive aggressive dig at the Professor pretty well!

![A blue-tinted flashback: a young man with pale lavender hair and green eyes looks down in profile in front of a window, as a hand holds out a dark rose. The subtitle reads "She hates it when the petals begin to fall."](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-22/image-3.png)

> <span class="color-red">あの人</span>は<span class="color-orange">花が散る</span>のを<span class="color-green">見たくないんですよ</span>。こうすれば短い命の花も少しは長持ちさせることができる。でも、<span class="color-blue">こうまでして</span><span class="color-pink">永らえても</span>花自身は嬉しいんでしょうか？

> <span class="color-red">She</span> <span class="color-green">hates it</span> when the <span class="color-orange">petals begin to fall</span>. This way even a short-lived flower can be made to last a little longer. But… I wonder how the flowers feel about their <span class="color-pink">existence being prolonged</span> <span class="color-blue">like this</span>.

This is one of my favourite exchanges in the show! I think this really emphasises how Japanese sentence structure and general way of phrasing things differs drastically from English. A literal translation of the first sentence would be something like “That person does not want to see flowers scatter”. Each part of the sentence sounds weird if translated individually:

- <span class="color-red">あの人</span> - “that person” being used because Japanese prefers that phrasing to third person pronoun “she”.
- <span class="color-orange">花が散る</span> - “flowers scatter” is the most natural way to say “petals fall/die” in Japanese but sounds very odd in English
- <span class="color-green">見たくないんですよ</span> - “does not want to see X” also sounds very funny when translated directly to English, but simply conveys the idea that she doesn’t like seeing something happen.

When taken as a whole, complete idea though, the sentence can easily be translated into something that sounds natural in English: “<span class="color-red">She</span> <span class="color-green">hates it</span> when the <span class="color-orange">petals begin to fall</span>.”

The hardest part of this to translate was the last sentence. I originally had translated it like this: “<span class="color-blue">So much work</span> to <span class="color-pink">make them last</span>, and yet I wonder if the flowers themselves are happy.”

- <span class="color-blue">こうまでして</span> - to go this far/to put in this much effort/to put in this much work. However, this is more of a tone setting phrase than a meaning carrying phrase, so it’s okay not to translate it directly.
- <span class="color-pink">永らえる</span> - to prolong something’s lifespan. This is hard to translate because while the phrasing works in Japanese, the preserved flowers are actually dead. So you can’t translate it as “I wonder how the flowers feel about being made to live so long” or something of that ilk. My original translation of “make them last” works, but I prefer the final translation of “existence being prolonged”. It sounds more applicable to Mamiya’s situation, and this exchange is all about that metaphor.

![The same pale-haired young man in profile in the blue-tinted flashback, with roses and a window behind him. The subtitle reads "We just romanticise the idea of eternity."](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-22/image-4.png)

> 永遠に憧れる心が美しく思えたりするだけだ。

> (lit.) We just think of a heart that longs for eternity as beautiful. (??)

> We just romanticise the idea of eternity.

Another great example of Japanese phrasing that sounds super weird when translated directly, but can sound very natural when the core idea behind the words is translated properly. “Romanticise” is such a succinct one-word translation of the several word phrase 「憧れる心が美しく思えたり」!
