My god, so far every Nanami epsode has been a nightmare to translate. So much tricky stuff and wordplay!

## Translation discussion:

![Utena and Miki stand by a school window while Saionji, in a black kendo top and purple hakama, walks past with his eyes closed and a hand on his chest. The subtitle reads "No one cares, dude."](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-6/image-1.png)

> Saionji: ちなみに僕とアンシーの愛は… <span class="color-green">しのぶ愛</span>。\
> Saionji: <span class="color-green">しのび愛</span>。\
> Utena: <span class="color-pink">誰も聞いてないよ</span>。

> Saionji: Anthy’s and my love takes the form of… <span class="color-green">Forbidden love</span>. \
> *\[7 full seconds of silence\]*\
> Saionji: …<span class="color-green">Forbidden. Love</span>.\
> Utena: <span class="color-pink">No one cares, dude</span>.

My absolute favourite moment of the whole episode, which is saying something! <span class="color-green">しのぶ</span> has two meanings in Japanese depending on the kanji. The more common meaning is “<span class="color-green">to be hidden</span>”, but the other meaning is “<span class="color-green">to long for something that was once close but now distant</span>”. I thought both applied — he’s showing the *hidden* exchange diary, but he has also *lost* Anthy. I originally went with “<span class="color-green">Lost love</span>” before [@dontbe-lasanya](https://tmblr.co/MRYPqo0RulLC0rahHPQsbIw) and I had a discussion about it and they suggested “<span class="color-green">Forbidden love</span>” which I really liked because I feel like it conveys both the need to stay hidden and the idea that it’s something he can’t have. It also makes him sound more in denial about the fact that it’s over between him and Anthy which I like a lot.

The other thing I love about this exchange was that it was an opportunity to let Utena use “dude” again. I really am looking for any possible opportunity to slip in some masculine turns of phrase since the 僕 self-gendering gets lost.

Astute viewers may have noticed that there’s only been one time Utena gets they/them’d so far — by Wakaba in the first episode. I promise you this is intentional and that it will ramp up! I’m planning on using the student council’s gendering of Utena to indicate their acceptance/understanding of Utena’s identity and their own personal growth. After their duel losses, they will each start using they/them for Utena. Miki is at that point now, but unfortunately he hasn’t had the chance to talk about Utena in third person yet.

—-

> 何故さっさと片付けない。<span class="color-red">あんな奴</span>、生かしておいてもためにならん。 (…) 今更計画の変更は出来ない。一思いに、殺してしまえ。

> Why haven’t you done it yet? We can’t let <span class="color-red">this walking problem</span> stay alive. (…) We’ve gone past the point of no return. Don’t think. Just kill.

Contrary to my obsession with Utena’s pronouns, this was an exercise in pronoun avoidance! “<span class="color-red">Walking problem</span>” saved me from having to use a clunky “<span class="color-red">them</span>” here, which Touga wouldn’t use for Nanami. I was also really happy with the “Don’t think. Just kill.” translation. It makes it sound super dramatic while also being plausible as something Touga would say about killing bugs.

—–

> Miki: How could she bring herself to want anyone else? Everyone else is worthless to her.\
> Utena: Worthless?\
> Miki: <span class="color-blue">Worthless. （かす）</span> <span class="color-orange">Disposable.（おまけ）</span> <span class="color-green">They’re parsley</span>.

The word Miki uses here is <span class="color-green">かす</span> which means like, scraps, sawdust, useless crap. Then he says <span class="color-orange">おまけ</span> which means something you get for free when you buy something else, often something you don’t need or want (hence, parsley). I struggled with the translation for <span class="color-orange">おまけ</span> for ages. It needed to be a joining word between “<span class="color-blue">worthless</span>” and “<span class="color-green">parsley</span>”, something that connected the two. What means “<span class="color-blue">worthless</span>” and “<span class="color-green">a garnish you throw away</span>” at the same time?! I must’ve spent 30 minutes looking through synonyms. In the end it was a toss up between “<span class="color-orange">disposable</span>” and “<span class="color-orange">unnecessary</span>”, but I felt disposable flowed a bit better. This line in particular I’m curious what other translators think of!
