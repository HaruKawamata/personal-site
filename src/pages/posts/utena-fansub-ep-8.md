> 生意気でブサイクで<span class="color-green">男女</span>の天上をめっためたに打倒して

> “I’ll rip apart that annoying ugly <span class="color-green">dyke</span>

<span class="color-green">男女</span> is a hard word to translate. It’s basically a slur - the word is made up of the kanji for “man” and then the kanji for “woman” (so, “manwoman”). I’ve seen “<span class="color-green">shemale</span>” used as a translation but the transness of that word is a bit strong. I’ve also seen “<span class="color-green">tomboy</span>” but that’s not a slur, it’s barely even an insult (and it’s not trans-y enough). Anya suggested to use “<span class="color-green">dyke</span>” if I was comfortable with it, and I am. I think it’s perfect honestly.

—–

> 私のお兄様があんた達に<span class="color-orange">ちょっかいを出す</span>はずないでしょ？

> My big brother would never be so <span class="color-orange">interested in you</span>.

<span class="color-orange">ちょっかいを出す</span> has two meanings according to the Japanese dictionary [weblio.jp](http://weblio.jp/). 1. to interfere with in a negative way by doing something that really shouldn’t be done. 2. to hit on a woman.

Obviously both meanings are important here. Nanami is obsessed with being the only girl her brother thinks about, and Touga is sexually interested in Utena and politically interested in marrying Anthy. I see this line as intentionally ambiguous, maybe even a bit of a freudian slip. I think I captured that ambiguity with “<span class="color-orange">interested in you</span>” - it could mean sexually/romantically interested, or it could mean interested in the sense of just like, living in someone’s head rent free like Anthy and Utena do in Nanami’s head.

—–

> <span class="color-blue">辛さ</span><span class="color-red">爆発木っ端みじん</span>、<span class="color-purple">幻のゾウが「パオーン」</span>、<span class="color-green">超辛九千億倍カレー</span>

> “<span class="color-blue">Spice So Extreme</span> <span class="color-red">You’ll be Blown to Smithereens</span> and <span class="color-purple">Hallucinate a Horde of Trumpeting Elephants</span>: <span class="color-green">900 Billion x Curry</span>.”

I HAVE A FEW THINGS TO SAY about this extremely stupid joke line.

Thing number 1: the way she says 「<span class="color-purple">幻のゾウがパオーン</span>」 in such a sad, sad voice made me cackle the first time I heard it. I’m not funny enough to translate the joke properly but suffice it to say, she’s essentially saying “ghost cows go moo” (but for elephants) in the saddest most apologetic tone possible and it kills me. In English elephants don’t really have a childish onomatopoeic noise word like “moo” so I had to settle for “trumpeting”.

I think the <span class="color-purple">幻のゾウがパオーン</span> line is a really interesting example of coherent Japanese grammar. Like, if we translate the whole line literally, look at how weird it sounds:

> <span class="color-red">Explode into smithereens</span>, <span class="color-purple">illusory elephants go “paōōōn”</span> <span class="color-blue">level spice</span>: <span class="color-green">Extreme spice 900 Billion x Curry</span>.

Like, the elephant part is literally just describing what noise an “<span class="color-purple">illusory elephant</span>” makes. Under English language rules, this is a non-sequitur, but in Japanese it makes sense and fits into the sentence because the implication that the spice makes the “<span class="color-purple">illusory elephants</span>” appear is enough. You don’t need to say the spice makes it appear!

Thing number 2: This is the only time in the episode we hear the full name of the 900 Billion x curry, and it’s important as a translator that I lay the groundwork for the interpretation that the elephants the girls encounter for the rest of the episode are hallucinations. I actually find it really weird that no other translation calls the elephants “<span class="color-purple">hallucinations</span>” or the spice “<span class="color-purple">hallucinogenic</span>”, because that’s the translation that makes the most sense for <span class="color-purple">幻</span> within this context IMO. <span class="color-purple">幻</span> can be translated as “<span class="color-purple">illusion</span>”, “<span class="color-purple">phantom</span>”, “<span class="color-purple">vision</span>”, and it essentially means “<span class="color-purple">something you’re seeing that isn’t actually there</span>”. Let’s look at some other translations:

> <span class="color-blue">So hot</span> <span class="color-red">it will blast you to smithereens</span> and <span class="color-purple">make phantom elephants trumpet loudly</span> (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> <span class="color-blue">So spicy</span> <span class="color-red">it’ll blow you to smithereens!</span> <span class="color-purple">Secret Pa-Ohn</span> <span class="color-green">ultra spicy nine billion-fold curry</span>. ←????????!!! (from internet archive video encoded subs)

and then a later line where they’re talking about the spice itself:

> By the way, have you heard of the <span class="color-purple">Phantom</span> 900 Billion X spice? (from [ohtori.nu](http://ohtori.nu/))

> By the way, do you know about the nine billion-fold <span class="color-purple">secret</span> spice? (from internet archive video encoded subs)

Like… what? The spice is clearly meant to be hallucinogenic. And don’t get me started on the internet archive video subs! Just totally opting out of translating it completely and going with “secret”! So boring and such a cop out. My only guess as to why these have been done like this is an attempt at censorship? Cause hallucinations = drugs? Well, I’m not pulling punches on my translation! I’m writing them the way I interpret the Japanese - if that includes swearing, sexual references, or drugs, so be it. The goal is to make the most authentic translation, not one that will actually be marketable to TV or streaming networks.

Speaking of…

![A hand holds open a diary with the large red handwritten Japanese 色ボケ across both pages, with the added English label "FUCKBOY" above it.](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-8/image-2.png)

This is the most egregious translation from the ohtori.nu scripts yet. They translated <span class="color-red">色ボケ</span> (a person, often an immature man/boy, obsessed with sex; vulgar, insult) as, get this, “Casanova”.

![Saionji stands alone in a dim, misty greenhouse lit by a shaft of light, his head tipped back and eyes closed in a daze, holding the open diary at his side.](/images/posts/utena-fansub-ep-8/image-3.png)

Does this look like a man who has just been called “casanova” by his crush?

“Fuckboy” was my first pick but I would also have accepted “wanker”, “jerkoff”, “sleezebag”, “douchebag”, “fuckwit”, “coomer” or simply “cunt”.
