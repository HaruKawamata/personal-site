The episode has very little dialogue, so it was actually the easiest to translate so far. This also means there aren’t as many things to comment on in the translation, so this will be a little briefer than usual.

それより or そんなことより is a phrase that’s often translated as “more importantly”. It’s used when you’re talking about something and want to move on to something you think is more important. But translating it as “more importantly” in every situation can sound a bit robotic. There are two examples in this episode where Highway Hag uses it, and I translated them very differently.

> わしの攻撃をくらったんだ。当分霊力は使えねえさ！それよりよりキンタマだ。

> She took a hit from me. Her spiritual power is gonna be dead for a while. {それより} the ball.

You could translate this as “more importantly, the ball”, and it would kind of work. However, I thought it was much more natural sounding to say “What about the ball?” The structure of the grammar isn’t important to preserve — what is important is that this is a sentence that marks a change of subject, and “What about the ball” fits better in that context.

![In a dark, ruined warehouse, the Highway Hag in the form of a white beckoning cat sits on a dark mound in the foreground, while Momo stands in the distance. The subtitle reads "What about the ball?"](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-7/image-1.png)

The other line was:

> Momo: よかったね、オカルン！一つのキンタマを見つかって！ \
> Okarun: 恥ずかしいわ、その言い方！ \
> Highway Hag: そんなことよりよぉ。 \
> Momo: どうした？ \
> Highway Hag: こいつ、死んでるぜ。

> Momo: Yay! Look Okarun, we found one of your balls! \
> Okarun: Don't say it like that! \
> Highway Hag: We've got a bit of a problem. \
> Momo: What's up? \
> Highway Hag: She’s dead.

![Okarun, in a torn school uniform, adjusts his glasses while holding a glinting golden ball, next to an equally battered Momo. The subtitle reads "We've got a bit of a problem."](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-7/image-2.png)

Here I translated it as “We’ve got a bit of a problem”. This is because in this context, the purpose of そんなことより is not simply to mark a subject change, but also to draw attention to a problem the other two weren’t aware of. They’re teasing and bickering, and Highway Hag is alerting them to something important they should know about. A more direct translation could be “We have something more important to worry about,” but I took this and reworded it so it sounded more natural. Just typing this out now, I think I’ve realised that one strategy I use in my translations is to write a more direct translation, then reword it to sound more natural. So while “We’ve got a bit of a problem” seems like a stretch to come from そんなことよりよぉ, when you think of it as a natural sounding rephrasing of the more direct “We have something more important to worry about” you can see how it works as a translation.

> Highway Hag: <span class="color-green">普通</span>！人間が妖怪に喰われたら死ぬもんさ。

> Okarun: I don’t believe it… \
> Highway Hag: <span class="color-green">Duh</span>. If a yokai eats a human, they're gonna die.

> Momo: オカルン、人工呼吸できる？ \
> Okarun: <span class="color-green">自信ないっす</span>！

> Momo: Okarun, you know mouth-to-mouth? \
> Okarun: <span class="color-green">Not really</span>!

> Momo: それってどうなるの？ \
