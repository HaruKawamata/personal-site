Since we’re still early on in the project, it might be a good time to discuss the episode titles. Each Dandadan episode name is written in a casual, spoken, teenager register, and often in a kind of humorous, incredulous manner. It’s as though Momo is saying it as a humour release valve to a scary scene. It’s important to translate these in Momo’s very real teenage voice over any other style.

What makes it so casual? Japanese is interesting in how certain grammatical structures explicitly signal membership of certain groups. English does this as well, to an extent, but Japanese is much more explicit about it. The following grammatical substitutions signal that the speaker belongs to the group of 若者 (young people) and is speaking to other young people in a casual register:

- って instead of は to mark topic
- じゃね？ instead of じゃない？ or じゃないの？
- じゃん as a confirmation seeking sentence ending particle, similar to ね
- じゃんよ to indicate an incredulous, rhetorical question
- なんか at the beginning of a sentence as a filler word and to indicate a level of uncertainty of the phrasing that follows, similar to English’s “like” or “kinda”

This episode’s official name is “That's a Space Alien, Ain't It?!”. Like I said, English also has ways of signalling membership with grammar, but we have less grammatical variation than Japanese and therefore certain words or styles end up being attributed to many different groups at once. Obviously, the official translation is attempting to use “ain’t it” to the same effect as the youth slang of the original Japanese, but to my ear, it more strongly signals redneck America than youth slang, especially when combined with the inexplicable phrase “space alien”, which is undeniably associated with the language of older generations (WHY on earth did they do this?!).

I have translated each of the episode titles in a way that aligns more with how I believe teenagers actually speak, and also with how Momo specifically speaks. In this case, I’ve translated the episode as “Is that a fucking alien?!”

![Momo crouches in a bedroom doorway, tugging on the trouser leg of someone in a green hoodie standing in the closet. The subtitle reads "Poke, poke."](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-2/image-2.png)

![Close up from behind of Okarun in his green hoodie, tilting his head back to drink from a bowl. The subtitle reads "Bitch."](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-2/image-3.png)

> Momo: あんたに友達がいないのはあんたが遠ざけてるからだろう。無視していいなんて言わないでさ。普通にオカルトの話しようぜ。おいー、なんか言え。ツンツン。\
> Okarun: …ブス。

> Momo: Maybe the reason you don't have any friends is cause you don't let anyone in. So don't tell me to ignore you. We can keep talking about occult stuff. Hey, say something. Poke, poke.\
> Okarun: …Bitch.

ブス literally translates to “ugly”, but Okarun isn’t using it for its direct meaning. He’s using it as a nasty gendered insult, in much the same way “bitch” is used in English. The scene continues:

![Momo and Okarun grapple furiously in a dark hallway, shouting in each other's faces. The subtitle reads "The fuck did you just say to me?!"](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-2/image-4.png)

> Momo: 今なんつったおらぁ！\
> Okarun: 綾瀬さんにはデリカシーがない！人の弱点をズケズケと！\
> Momo: デリカシーってなんじゃああ！

> Momo: The fuck did you just say to me?!\
> Okarun: You have no savoir faire! You go right for the insecurities!\
> Momo: What the fuck is "savoir faire"?!

Japanese doesn’t really have swear words in the same way English does, which requires the translator to decide what the purpose of swear words is, in order to decide where they should be placed in the translation. This is non-exhaustive, but I believe swear words are primarily used to 1. emphasise anger, 2. emphasise humour, and 3. emphasise lack of formality or respect.

Here, despite the Japanese having no explicit swear words, Momo’s なんつったおらぁ！ is an example of all 3 of these emphasised tones. なんつった is a supremely casual way of saying “what did you say” and おらぁ is a comically exaggerated way of expressing anger. If anywhere was the right place for a “fuck”, it’s here!

Side note: among high-school kids, #3 is the most common reason for swearing in general conversation — it shows the listener that the speaker believes they are peers, on equal terms of formality, and that it is a very low level of formality. In other words, they’re friends! I use this as my rationale for many of the casual swears throughout the series.

Okarun follows this by using an uncommon English loanword — デリカシー (delicacy; meaning that Momo is not exercising the proper amount of discretion for such a sensitive topic). I believe it’s implied that Okarun has knowledge of semi-obscure English words because of all the alien content he reads, much of which would originate from America. But it could also just be implied that he’s a nerd with a large vocabulary because he reads a lot. Either way, this scene wouldn’t work properly without using a loanword from a different language in the translation, and luckily there was one that fit the bill perfectly. Exact same meaning (i.e. social discretion), a similar level of unfamiliarity, but also exists as an established loanword: savoir faire!

![A greyscale scene on cracked ground: Momo faces Okarun, possessed by the Highway Hag, with flaming red and white hair and a wide toothy grin. The subtitle, in a spooky serif font with a red outline, reads "I deadass feel like total shit right now."](/images/posts/dandadan-fansub-ep-2/image-5.png)

> Curse Okarun: I'm still fully in control, but bro… I deadass feel like total shit right now.

It’s important that the audience gets tonal whiplash from Okarun’s usual speech pattern and his cursed speech pattern. His cursed form speaks in an exaggeratedly youth-like and slang-filled register, which contrasts against Momo’s realistically youthful register and Okarun’s usual stiff and formal register. I also chose to (when appropriate) translate his habitual sentence-ending 「よぉぉ」 as “bro”, in order to give him an English equivalent speech signature.
